Rulers & perspective

Ruler is a tool. It is in the tool strip, last of the tools that build something on the page, just before Move view. Pick it and its Sub Tool list shows Create ruler — twelve rows, one per kind of ruler. Click a row, then drag on the canvas: that drag is the ruler. The tool stays in your hand, so the next drag is the next ruler.

The Ruler tool

The twelve sub tools, in list order: Straight line, Curve, Parallel line, Radial line, Concentric circle, Vanishing point, Perspective 1-point, Perspective 2-point, Perspective 3-point, Symmetrical, Guide horizontal, Guide vertical. Hovering a row says what its gesture is; the status bar says the same thing when you pick it.
U cycles Figure → Frame border → Ruler. CSP's own U, and the third press lands on the ruler row you were last on. Every row can also have a key of its own: in Preferences ▸ Shortcuts, or by hand in keys.json, the target is tool: Ruler / Create ruler / Curve (and tool: Ruler / Create ruler for the tab, tool: Ruler for the tool). , and . step through the twelve rows like any other tool's sub tools.
The per-kind numbers live in Tool Property. Hold the Ruler tool and the palette shows what the row in hand needs and nothing else: the symmetry line count for Symmetrical, the ring spacing for Concentric circle, a 1/2/3-point picker for the perspective rows. Snapping and special-ruler snapping are there too, with a count of how many rulers this page carries.
Layer ▸ Ruler still works, and it is the same twelve rows in the same order. A menu pick now selects the Ruler tool with that row lit, so the menu is a shortcut into the tool rather than a second way of doing the same thing. The difference: a menu pick arms one gesture even if you then switch tools, while holding the tool arms every gesture until you put it down. The menu also keeps the two ladders that act on rulers ALREADY on the page — Ring spacing (cycle) and Symmetry lines (cycle).

The creation drags

A perspective set is made by dragging its EYE LEVEL — except the 1-point set, which is dragged FROM its vanishing point. That one difference is the whole of the three gestures:
RulerThe drag
Perspective (1-point)press ON the vanishing point, drag along the eye level — the press places the VP, the release sets the horizon's tilt
Perspective (2-point)drag the eye level; both ends become vanishing points
Perspective (3-point)the same eye-level drag, and the third (vertical) VP drops on its own
The third VP lands on the side the drag points to. Dragging the horizon left→right puts it BELOW the eye level (the high, looking-down angle); right→left puts it above (looking up). It is placed on the perpendicular through the middle of the horizon, at about three quarters of the horizon's length — a starting position, not an opinion. It is an anchor, so the Object tool drags it where the shot actually wants it.
A short drag creates nothing and says so. Under about 32 px a perspective drag is refused with the instruction in the status bar rather than dropping a degenerate set on the page. With the Ruler TOOL in hand you simply drag again — the message tells you what the drag should have been. A refused drag made from the MENU spends its one arming, so that one means picking the row again.
Perspective sets do not snap to a nearest line — they bind. There is no discrete family of rulings to land on: a stroke picks one family from its early direction (rays through either VP, or the verticals) and then rides that one member for the whole stroke, exactly like a pen in the groove of a physical perspective ruler. A 1-point set's three families are orthogonals, horizontals and verticals; a 3-point set has no parallel family at all, which is what makes its verticals converge.

The special rulers

Parallel line is the speed-line ruler (流線): its two handles AIM the family, they are not one of its lines. Drag the direction once, then every stroke comes out on-direction where you drew it — the member of the family through the point the pen started at, held for the whole stroke. That is what makes it a hatching ruler: twenty strokes across a panel are twenty parallel lines, not twenty passes over one.
Radial line is the focus-line ruler (集中線), and it is made with a CLICK. The click is the centre; every stroke afterwards runs along the line through it. Unlike the vanishing-point ruler it is a continuum — it has no ray count, so no angle is unreachable and a fan of speed lines never combs into a fixed number of directions. It binds the way a perspective set binds: the ray through the point the pen started at is fixed for that stroke. Start a stroke exactly ON the centre and it takes the direction you are already heading.
Concentric circles: click for a FREE radius, drag to set a ring spacing. The click is the version CSP has — each stroke keeps whatever radius it started at, so you draw any circle around that centre. The drag makes rings at a fixed spacing instead, and every stroke is pulled onto the nearest one. Ring spacing (cycle) in the same menu walks the spacing through free / 25 / 50 / 100 / 200 px afterwards, so a ruler made with the wrong gesture does not have to be redrawn.
Both are grabbed by their centre. Their drawn geometry — the ray fan, the rings — is a faint SAMPLE of a family that covers the page, so it is not a grab target, for the same reason a perspective set is grabbed by its eye level alone.

Moving a ruler

Every ruler moves with the Object tool: grab an anchor to re-aim that end, grab the body to translate the whole thing. A translated ruler is rigid — a line keeps its direction, a perspective set keeps its horizon length — because only the anchors let you change the shape. A guide takes only the component it lives on.
A perspective set is grabbed by its EYE LEVEL only. The faint ray fans are decoration; if they were grab targets the whole page would be one. The 3-point set's vertical VP is an anchor, and anchors are tested first, so it stays grabbable without widening the body.
Rulers are grabbed before the art under them. With the Object tool, a press within the handle tolerance takes the ruler — but only within it, so a press that is not on a ruler still reaches the panel or balloon underneath. The status bar names which of the two you got.

Snapping, and what Delete all rulers keeps

Snap: OFF leaves the rulers drawn. The geometry stays on the page and keeps its handles; only the pull is gone. Special rulers has its own switch (parallel, concentric, guide, symmetry) and turning that off does not touch line, curve or vanishing-point rulers.
Delete all rulers keeps the panel-border rulers. A panel border published as a ruler (right-click a frame folder's badge ▸ Border off — outline becomes a ruler, see Comic) belongs to its FRAME, not to the ruler list — the frame retracts it when you ink the border back. So the command clears every ruler you made by hand and says panel-border rulers stay with their frames when there were any. To be rid of one, turn its border back on.

Attaching rulers to a layer

An attached ruler works ONLY on its layer. Rulers ▸ Attach rulers to this layer binds every ruler to the active layer: they snap, draw and grab only while that layer is the one you are drawing on — per-panel guides on a multi-panel page, with no cross-panel snapping. Rulers ▸ Make rulers page-wide unbinds; one undo each way. The binding is by layer POSITION in v1 — restructure the stack and re-attach by hand. Curve rulers stay page-wide for now.
